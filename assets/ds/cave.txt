# The Secret Place -- Nintendo DS.  32 x 16 tiles = 512 x 256 pixels.
#
# The waterfall cave, split out of the island map into a room of its own.  In the
# PS2 game this is a separate load: you squeeze through a gap in the rock beside
# the fall, follow a low passage, and it opens into the chamber with the drawings
# and the Door.  The island map keeps the PASSAGE MOUTH -- the cave floor at
# (6..11, 5..6) that tools/check_map.py's frozen DS_ROUTES pins as "the cave
# passage" -- and the doorway at its far end leads here.
#
# # cliff +3     C cave floor   * the dark (blocked, backdrop colour)
#
# NO 'd' DOORWAY YET.  The way in and out is authored in the same increment that
# teaches tools/build_doors.py the difference between a district and a room; see
# assets/ds/cave_cast.txt.  Until then the chamber is reached with L/R.
#
# THE WALLS ARE THE DARK AND NOT ROCK, and that is the whole difference between
# a cave and a quarry.  '#' is a cliff at +3: the painter draws it 24 pixels
# above its own cell with a lit face filling the gap, which is exactly right for
# a headland seen from overhead and exactly wrong for the inside of a hill -- the
# first draft of this map walled the chamber in '#' and came out as a lit stone
# plateau with a hole in it.  '*' is palette index 0, the backdrop: blocked,
# height zero, one character, no palette entry.  A cave whose walls are simply
# absent is what a top-down cave has always been.
#
# The one exception is the BACK WALL, rows 1-2, which is '#' so that the three
# drawings have a rock face to be drawn on.  They are AF_FLAT -- height zero, so
# they land on the face of the tile behind them rather than on top of it -- and a
# flat sprite against the void would be a drawing hanging in mid-air.  Every '#'
# here has two rows of blocked ground north of it, which is what +3 requires, and
# there is no '#' anywhere SOUTH of walkable floor: a raised tile covers 8*h
# pixels of whatever is north, so rock below the floor would paint over the floor.
#
# The shape is the PS2's, which describes it as "a short tunnel curving into a
# round chamber with the mysterious closed wooden door at the back": in from the
# south-east, north up a short neck, west along a low corridor, and the chamber
# opens out at the far end.  Coming in, the drawings are the last thing you see
# and the Door is among them.
#
